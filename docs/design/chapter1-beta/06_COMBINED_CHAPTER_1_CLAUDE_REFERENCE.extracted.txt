original PROPOSALS, not approved canon, shipped features or completed assets.
Playable goal: A polished, coherent single-player, third-person 3D beta that tells a beginning-to-
end Ruby rescue story, from the child's colorless bedroom to the first spectacular restoration. Beta
duration: about 20–30 minutes depending on exploration and retries. Future complete Chapter
1: 55–80 minutes main path plus 20–35 minutes optional, as proposed on page 8 of the expanded
Bible. These are planning targets, not measured timings.
Premise: Ruby is a gentle dragon caught in a spell that magnifies fear of making mistakes. Her
uncontrolled berry magic makes her orchard unsafe. The child helps her be brave enough to try
again. Emotional theme: Courage without perfection. No one is attacked to cause injury: the
goal is to break the three imprisoning crystals. The child can make mistakes and still succeed.

No pay-to-win. Full story, all Lord powers, all standard pets and every functional traversal
ability must be earnable free.
Seven shades included. Buying one legendary pet cosmetic includes seven freely
selectable color palettes; never sell its palette colors separately.
A real free path to one premium cosmetic. The proposed 600-Gem legendary skin must
be obtainable by a reasonably achievable, permanent F2P path. The full Bible models an
early 660-Gem pathway; DO NOT implement real purchases in this beta.
Childlike 3D heroes. Young-looking, chubby/cute, rounded and soft-edged; neither adult
realism nor glossy anime portraits.
Legendary means cosmetic spectacle. Redesigned silhouette, outfit, motions and VFX
can be much richer but must match the world's material language and NEVER improve
gameplay statistics.
Bosses keep moving. In default Adventure mode, readable attacks continue while the child
rotates mirrors, paints symbols or solves puzzles; choose Story assist for gentle pacing or
hazard pause.
Teach then reuse. Every new mechanic has an obvious tutorial, an easy test, a combination
test and later mastery use. Double jump and grappling recur in future chapters, not earned
here.
Rescue, do not injure. Color Lords are spell victims, not villains. The Fade is helped, not
killed.
Solo first. Season One is a solo adventure; optional co-op, hard rematches and miniature
Lord pets belong only to possible Season Two.
Creator approves. Concept art, numbers, prices, calendars and added worldbuilding are
proposals until explicitly approved.
Additional constraints: Inclusive natural skin tones stay natural when scenery is grayscale; text
+ distinct symbols accompany color; save checkpoints; subtitles; no public child chat, photos, ad
tracking, or shop in beta.

In the bedroom: curiosity and a small mystery, never horror.
With Pip: warmth, humor, safety and companionship.
On first sight of Ruby: a large magical spectacle, but Ruby is visibly frightened rather than
threatening.
During the orchard: constantly changing scale and tactile, readable 3D interactions; giant
berries, slippery jam, wobbly bridges, mirrored rays.
During the boss: exhilaration from movement and puzzle timing. The player is rescuing
someone while the environment misbehaves.
At the restoration: a unique, emotionally satisfying payoff with permanently changed 3D
scenery, music and clothing details.

P00 — THE FIRST BRUSHSTROKE | Title and character choice

Weather/time: Late morning; soft overcast with a tiny ray cutting through light mist. Breezes sway
enormous berry stems. Occasional pollen/dust motes float; colors remain carefully muted. Giant
branches frame the horizon, with a distant jam mill and crystal grove. The orchard should look

Camera: crane over cracked jam-wheel and berry stepping-stones; Ruby is glimpsed in the

R02 — BERRY TRAIL: SOMETHING IS ROLLING | Exploration + hazard
tutorial
Level shape: A readable S-curve through giant fruit stalks. One safe stepped platform, one rolling-
berry lane, one short duck-alcove, two large jumping pads; camera retains view of landing targets.
Props include knitted twine rope fences and tiny jam taps. Two enemy archetypes are Bouncing

Gameplay rhythm: (1) see a large berry roll on a distant loop; (2) wait for a symbol-marked

lane; (5) use Wonderbrush tap to open a gate.
PIP: “Follow the round berry stamps!”
EXPLORER: “We can get close without scaring her.”
Optional Ink Twig dialogue: It squeaks “Oh!” and recoils when a Wonderbrush tap removes its
sticky gray spell tag; no enemy death animation. Ink Twig returns to watering a plant.

R04 — JAM-JAR JESTER | Nonviolent mini-boss, active playground
puzzle
Encounter space: A semicircular jam mill courtyard with three giant bowls (left circle icon, middle
triangle, right star), waist-high safety islands and a bell tower. A whimsical, bouncy jam jar
character in an oversized paper crown blocks the exit.
JESTER: “No paint beyond this point!”
PIP: “Oh! A jar with a very large opinion!”
JESTER: “I have a job: KEEP. EVERYTHING. TIDY.”
EXPLORER: “Maybe tidy doesn't have to mean stuck.”
Mechanic: Jester lobs bouncy jam blobs with clear landing indicators, one every 7–9 seconds in
Adventure. Three marked pots require a bounced projectile to land inside. During 2–3-second
windows, a Wonderbrush-tapped plate redirects one blob into the chosen bowl. Each correct bowl
locks and shows an icon + audible marimba note; incorrect shots harmlessly bounce away, no lost
progress.
Three outcomes: first bowl teaches; second introduces alternating blob lanes; third introduces a
platform jump but keeps all three bowls readable. No attack animation resembling painful injury;
upon success the Jester bursts into harmless gray berry mist, reappears smiling with a little rubber
duck, and steps aside.
JESTER: “Well! That was rather less tidy... and much more fun.”
RUBY (far away): “I do not want to keep making messes.”
Save: jam_jar_jester_clear. QA: Jester clear persists after reload; bowl puzzle cannot get
permanently stuck by projectile physics.

R05 — RUBY BOSS, PHASE 1: THE BOUNCING BERRY PARADE
Arena overview: Circular 26–34-meter provisional gameplay space, three safe islands, one wide
entry portal, three differently shaped crystal pedestals and clear sky camera lanes. Ruby is in the
background, never a hittable target. Use broad collision and generous traversal space. The camera
must show the first crystal and berry threat together.
PIP: “Ruby! We're here to help, not to hurt you!”
RUBY: “Oh, berries! Please stop bouncing over my friends!”
EXPLORER: “I can paint the crystal after the bounce!”
Objective: Safely avoid 2–3 telegraphed berry landings, then approach the triangle-marked spell
crystal and use a short brush tap. Berry hazards run independently while crystal interaction is
active. Each berry warning: visible patterned landing circle -> sound -> descending fruit -> soft
bounce -> 2–3-second safe action gap. Phase1 passes only after validated brush hit + crystal
opening cinematic, not raw proximity.
Progress feedback: Crystal opens like a paper flower, one crystal icon fills in the HUD, Ruby's tail
relaxes.
Checkpoint: ruby_phase_1. Optional hint after 3 failed dodges; never reset the crystal once saved.
R06 — RUBY BOSS, PHASE 2: THREE MIRRORS, MANY BERRIES

exit gate. Puzzle and hazard state machines run at the same time; opening an interact tooltip does
NOT suspend hazard simulation in default Adventure mode.
Phase entry: 2-second overhead preview shows three mirror locations, then immediately returns
to playable chase view; player is NEVER stuck in prolonged cinematic while attacks continue.
PIP: “Turn the mirrors, but keep an eye on those berry circles!”
RUBY: “You can do two things at once. I believe in you!”

R07 — RUBY BOSS, PHASE 3: THE THREE STOMPS
Escalation: Ruby's worried spell causes THREE large, slow, radiating shockwave rings, not angry
attacks. Shapes on floor indicate timing; three low berry ledges provide safe hopping. Final spell
crystal has a star icon; a large interaction prompt appears after third wave.
RUBY: “My biggest wiggle is coming!”
EXPLORER: “And my brushstroke is ready!”
Mechanic: Player jumps or stands on a clearly marked berry ledge to avoid wave one; repeats with
wave two; dashes to final marked safe spot as wave three passes. No exact-frame execution. The

calm pulse bars.

three open-flower icons.

cosmetic transition; scene must recover if the player closes the browser mid-cinematic.

Status: Original art-direction PROPOSAL derived from the Expanded Development Bible, especially

environments, characters or shapes.
1. ONE-SENTENCE LOOK
A charming, premium-feeling handcrafted toy-scale 3D fantasy that starts as a beautifully
textured pearl-gray orchard and becomes a glowing tapestry of ripe ruby-red fruit when the child
rescues a gentle dragon — lush and cinematic, but readable and safe for children ages five to ten.
2. The non-negotiable visual style contract
•
•
•
•
•
•
Hero proportions 2.7–3.2 heads tall; oversized head and expressive eyes, softly rounded
chubby cheeks/limbs, broad friendly silhouette. Both explorer options have exactly matched
movement hitboxes and scale.
Rounded, bevelled shapes; cloth/felt stitches; painted-plastic, sculpted-clay, subtle paper and
polished wood. Material variation is vital: the world should not look like a collection of default
spheres on a flat plane.
Rich detail by layering silhouette, composition, textures, decals, parallax and
lighting, rather than cluttering paths or adding huge amounts of high-frequency detail.
No realistic pores, photoreal adults, anime fashion models, glossy human anatomy, sharp
threatening teeth, gore or oversaturated neon across the whole view.
Legendary pet skins may later be highly elaborate, but every standard pet gives the same
functional ability for free and seven palettes are included when a legendary skin is ever
acquired.
No green grass, brilliant blue skies or yellow sunflowers physically restored in Chapter 1; new
Red is earned only at Ruby's restoration. Natural character skin tones remain natural
throughout, as do small accessible UI highlights.
3. World-scale and composition language
A child explorer is about 1.0 relative world unit in this design model (actual engine scale should
be consistent, not forced). Berry trees loom 6–9 child-heights high; berries are huge enough to act
as rolling scenery, not photoreal fruit. Bridges are giant leaf mats stitched with rope. Houses are
lumpy, clay-sculpted towers with round windows and thick trim; pathways arc in S- and C-shaped

6. The two Explorers
Shared rig/collider: identical overall height, pivot placement, foot contact, reach distance, jump
arc, dash timing and camera framing. Personality-neutral story dialogue.
Boy concept: round head, expressive brows, soft nose, chubby-cheek silhouette, practical baggy
hoodie or short jacket, cuffed trousers, chunky sneakers, small sash for restored Red. Hairstyle
variations must not force a gender stereotype.
Girl concept: matching round head and scale; soft stylized curls or braid option; practical jacket/
overalls and chunky sneakers; mirrored sash location, identical grip on Wonderbrush. Avoid adult
silhouette, makeup, long fashion heels and exaggerated eye makeup. A blank 2D earlier reference
should never be accepted as a 3D model or finished turntable.
Both: natural inclusive skin tones not subject to color loss; before restoration, clothing begins
desaturated. Start with 3 free outfits in the eventual full creator; beta may use 1 approved hero
outfit each and not falsely present unmade customization options. Expression set: wonder /
concern / courage / laughing / pride. Animation set: idle breath, walk, jog, start-stop, jump, land,
dash, tap, sweep, turn mirror, bounce reaction, hug/high five, victory.
Camera and image approval: for each hero: front, side, back, 3/4 turntable with the SAME lens/
lighting/background; expression sheet; in-game scale comparison beside Pip and Ruby; gameplay-
distance full-scene image; silhouette test in grayscale. No asset approved from a single glossy
poster.

7. Pip — the small companion that carries the heart
Shape: 0.35 child-heights, floating soft-bristle brush with short wooden body, ring eyes, eyebrows
sculpted like brush bristles, optional two tiny gloved hands, flexible paint drop tail. Colors remain
restrained: natural warm wood can exist, but magical pigment locked. Hover motion small and
rhythmic; avoid covering sightlines during jumps or mirrors. 5 readable expressions: intrigued,
concerned, excited, pointing, relieved. Pip's suggestion halo uses an icon and subtle outline rather
than a flash.
8. Ruby — the character centerpiece
Neutral/corrupted: a plump, friendly dragon like a soft bean-shaped plush; thick rounded muzzle,
large eyes, cushiony paws, little horn buds, soft broad wings. Her body is mostly gray-lilac and
dusted by a pale cloudy magical seal; the three spell crystals hover at a distance without piercing or
injuring her. Face conveys worried perfectionism and gentle apology, not aggression.
Restored: same mesh and recognizably same face! Soft berry-red scales, salmon underside, coral
wing membranes with embroidered-looking vein patterns, tiny warm glow on cheek and horns,
joyful full-wing stretch. Height ~2–2.8 child heights (provisional for framing); collider is non-damage
and separate from arena hazards. Animation set: frightened curl, eye glance, trembling wings, soft
berry hiccup, head tilt, worried wave, relief stretch, smile, happy wave. She cannot be hit with a
damaging attack; only separate crystals have interactive receivers.
Art approval requires corrupted/restored front/side/back/three-quarter, profile next to both heroes,
gameplay-distance (not just close portrait), wing stretch storyboard, and VFX-off readability.
9. Jam-Jar Jester and ordinary hazards
Jester: original stubby jar silhouette, paper crown, jam-splatter bowtie, eyes inside clear jar front,
rubbery rounded base, comical spring hop. Gray before Ruby. Friendly moving target, never a
person to harm. Three bowls and icon markings show where bounces must land. Its “defeat”
animation is a pop of harmless berry mist followed by relief.
Bouncing Berry: oversized round fruit with big stamped circle/stripe symbol, no angry face; path
predictable and telegraphed. Ink Twig: felt-stick with worried doodled eyes and soft leaves; uncurl
when a spell tag is brushed away. All characters need neutral + 3/4 + telegraph sheet.

11. 3D production practicality
Every image concept is only a guide. Production needs meshes with sensible topology, UV mapping,
textured materials, rig/animations for characters, LODs or culling when appropriate, collision
proxies, compressed textures, GLB/glTF export, and runtime tests. Start with approved art sheets,
then inexpensive greybox, then one showcase environment pass, not thirty disconnected glamorous
stills. Build modular scenery, use instancing for fruit and leaves, cap dynamic shadows/lights to
tested desktop budgets. Frame rate target only after real profiling. Prioritize camera clarity, collision
and responsiveness over cinematic heavy rendering.
12. Camera language and cinematography
•
•
•
•
•
•

Gameplay camera: child-height third-person follow, softly damped, collision-aware, minimum
vertical angle to keep jumps visible, avoids having huge berries cover the hero.
Discovery: long, curved reveal of Ruby framed by branches; no repeated giant cinema cuts.
Puzzles: slight shoulder pan to show target; ghost beam always world-space; rotatable
camera while hazards remain active.
Mini-boss: broad 3/4 framing of all three pots, not a zoom on the Jester's face.
Boss: cinematic intro with safely paused hazards; over-the-shoulder play with crystal and

reduced path. At least one in-engine uncut shot must prove real material recoloring, not pre-
rendered footage.
13. UI design and accessibility
Round translucent parchment-like UI surfaces with sufficient contrast, gentle pop animation; large
outlined lettering, friendly icon labels. HUD = (upper left) current task, (upper right) three crystal/
flower phase icons, (bottom right) context brush / dash / future Flame Burst, (bottom center) Pip
hint line. Text must remain readable against gray/ red. Both in-game world markers and UI icons
mark warning zones. An animated hazard is never communicated by red vs gray alone. Include
subtitle backgrounds, font sizing, camera sensitivity, reduced motion, brightness, and Story/
Adventure/Brave choices. Keep no ads, external chat or purchasing in beta.

distance, shows multiple material types, consistent light direction, original craftsmanship and
spectacular but controlled red restoration.
